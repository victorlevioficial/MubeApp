# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Mube serves Brazilian musicians, solo professionals, bands, studios, producers, and contractors who need to find reliable musical collaborators and opportunities without depending on noisy social groups or generic networks.

Primary users are often working from a phone, between rehearsals, gigs, studio sessions, classes, production work, or event planning. They need to discover compatible people, present a credible musical profile, apply to gigs, chat directly, and keep track of promising contacts.

The app must work for the bedroom musician, the working band leader, the independent studio, and the event contractor without making any group feel secondary or over-formalized.

## Product Purpose

Mube exists to connect the Brazilian music scene through focused discovery, search, profiles, gigs, chat, stories, favorites, and matching. Success means users can move from intent to a real musical contact or opportunity with less friction than WhatsApp groups, Facebook posts, Instagram DMs, or informal referral chains.

The product should make the next useful action clear: complete a profile, find people nearby, discover a compatible match, publish or apply to a gig, start a conversation, or revisit saved profiles.

## Brand Personality

The brand is direct, authentic, and professionally grounded in the music scene.

The voice should feel close to Brazilian musicians and music workers: clear, practical, inclusive, confident, and free of corporate language. It can be energetic, but it should not become noisy, generic, or artificially motivational.

Good Mube copy sounds like a product built by people who understand the scene and respect the user's time.

## Anti-references

Mube should not look or sound like a generic social network, a corporate SaaS dashboard, a nightlife flyer, or a dating app clone.

Avoid visual and UX patterns that create noise without helping the user's next action: decorative card grids, generic hero-metric layouts, vague "community" messaging, excessive gradients, overused glass effects, and UI that hides practical information behind spectacle.

Avoid language that feels imported from startup decks or broad creator-economy products. The app should not rely on generic terms when the music-scene context can be named plainly.

## Design Principles

1. Serve the next musical action.
Every screen should help the user discover, evaluate, contact, apply, publish, save, or complete something concrete.

2. Make credibility easy to scan.
Profiles, gigs, stories, and search results should surface the signals that matter to music work: role, location, style, availability, portfolio, links, media, and social proof where available.

3. Respect the scene without imitating noise.
The interface can carry energy and personality, but it should stay legible, focused, and practical. Mube organizes the scene; it should not recreate the chaos of social groups.

4. Keep the product inclusive by default.
The app should work for musicians, bands, studios, and contractors at different levels of professionalism, from first projects to paid opportunities.

5. Reuse the real design system before inventing.
New product surfaces should use the existing Flutter design system, route structure, Portuguese UI language, and established component patterns unless a feature clearly needs a reusable addition.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA as the practical baseline for new UI work.

Support high contrast, readable text hierarchy, reduced motion, and interaction states that do not depend on color alone. Keep touch targets usable on mobile and make empty, error, loading, offline, and permission states understandable in Portuguese.

Design decisions should account for users on varied devices, network conditions, lighting environments, and levels of familiarity with professional music tooling.
